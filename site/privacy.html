Overflow Scout

Privacy policy

Effective 6 November 2026, for Overflow Scout 1.0 and later.

Overflow Scout collects no data. It has no servers, no analytics and no tracking, and it makes no network requests. Everything it does happens in your browser.

What it reads, and when

It works on a web page only after you click its toolbar button, press its keyboard shortcut or use its right-click menu on that tab, or after you choose "Always allow on this site" for that site. It then reads the page's layout and text to find text that doesn't fit, in your browser. Nothing it reads leaves your device.

What it keeps on your device

  • Your settings (theme, default test options, whether to check form fields).
  • Results you choose to ignore: the site's address and a short description of the element, so they stay hidden on that site.
  • Which tab you last opened the panel from, so a panel that is still opening knows where to work. This lasts only until you close the browser.

These use Chrome's extension storage on this device only. They are never synced, and they are removed when you uninstall the extension.

Reports and screenshots

Reports, JSON exports and screenshots are made only when you ask for one. They go to your clipboard or your downloads folder, and nowhere else. If you paste one into a bug tracker, that tracker's own policy applies.

Changes to pages

When you test with longer text, preview a fix or try other text, Overflow Scout changes the page in your browser only, and puts it back exactly when you're done or close the panel. It never changes anything on the website's servers.

Permissions

It asks for access to the tab you click (activeTab), to run its scanner there (scripting), to show its side panel (sidePanel), to store the settings above (storage), and to add its right-click menu item (contextMenus). Access to a whole site is optional and asked for only when you choose "Always allow on this site"; you can remove it in Settings at any time.

Contact

Questions about privacy: open an issue on GitHub, or write to the developer address shown on the extension's Chrome Web Store page.
